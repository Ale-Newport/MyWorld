# Bruno parity audit

How Alejandro Newport's `/world` compares with Bruno Simon's `folio-2025`, what
the difference actually is, and what has to change.

- **Reference, live:** <https://bruno-simon.com/>
- **Reference, source:** <https://github.com/brunosimon/folio-2025> (MIT), studied
  at commit `41046b57eeed8d156d9c3fd7fa259900baef7816`
- **Audited build:** the working tree at the time of writing, served from
  `next dev`, driven in Chromium
- **Attribution:** unchanged. See [`THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`](./THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md)
  and [`src/world/vendor/LICENSE-folio-2025.md`](./src/world/vendor/LICENSE-folio-2025.md).

## How this audit was produced

Not from filenames, and not from memory.

1. **The reference was played.** `bruno-simon.com` was loaded in headless
   Chromium with WebGPU enabled, entered, and driven. Its in-game map was
   opened and captured — that single screenshot is the most informative
   artifact in this whole exercise, because it shows the macro composition
   at a glance.
2. **The reference source was read.** The repository was checked out locally
   and eleven subsystem clusters were read in full — not skimmed — down to the
   constants. Those notes live outside the repo (they quote upstream source at
   length) and back every "Bruno" cell below.
3. **This world was played.** All 19 districts were visited at the real
   gameplay camera, not a pulled-back review camera, because the review camera
   flatters the map.
4. **Density was measured, not eyeballed.** [`scripts/world-density.mjs`](./scripts/world-density.mjs)
   counts rendered instances within a radius of the car at every district.

Reference screenshots are Bruno's copyrighted visuals and are deliberately
**not** committed here. They were held outside the repo and described.

## The finding that explains everything else

The world is **too big for its content**, by an order of magnitude.

| | folio-2025 | `/world` | Ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Playable extent | 192 × 192 units, x,z ∈ [-96, +96] (`Terrain.js:18`, confirmed by the Rapier heightfield scale in `Floor.setPhysical()`) | circle of `WORLD_RADIUS = 360` ([`src/content/world.ts:372`](./src/content/world.ts#L372)) | — |
| Playable area | ~36,900 m² | ~407,000 m² | **11×** |
| Major activity areas | 13 `Area` classes, plus a circuit occupying roughly half the map | 19 districts + 12 play spots | ~1.2× |
| Area per activity | ~2,800 m² | ~13,100 m² | **4.7×** |

Bruno's world is small and packed. This one is large and thin. Every other
symptom — the empty roads, the featureless lawns, the "add more trees" instinct —
is downstream of that one ratio.

**A second, structural point.** `folio-2025` does not describe its world in
code. `World/Areas/Areas.js` dispatches over `resources.areasModel.scene.children`;
trees, bushes and flowers are instanced onto hand-authored empties parsed by
`References.js`; the ground is an authored RGB mask (R = paving, G = grass,
B = water depth) plus a heightfield GLB. `static/` is gitignored and absent.

So the mechanics are readable and portable, and the *world design* is not
copyable at all — it is an asset that has to be authored. That is the actual
shape of this task: the code is a reference, the level design is original work.

## Density, measured

`node scripts/world-density.mjs http://localhost:3001 60` — rendered instances
within 60 m of the car, at each district's own respawn:

```
hub 333 · lab 796 · kcl 974 · ucl 1048 · teaching 799 · algorithms 590
focus 374 · gym 1282 · client 1392 · chess 1292 · stock 700 · circuit 451
labyrinth 739 · voxel 2958 · network 2464 · studio 1404 · orbit 736
archive 1081 · void 64

median 799, min 64, max 2958
```

Two things are wrong with this picture, and only one of them is the number.

**The spawn is the emptiest place a visitor will stand.** `hub` is 333 — second
lowest of nineteen. In `folio-2025` the landing area is the *densest* part of
the map: grass, bushes, several trees, lanterns, fences, a bench, a signposted
kiosk, crates, the physical name, and water with a pier, all inside the opening
frame. The first thing a visitor sees here is a pale disc with eight cones on it.

**The count is spread too thin to read.** 799 instances inside a 60 m radius is
0.07 per m². Upstream's grass alone runs at roughly **21 blades per m²** in a
camera-following field of 78,400 blades (`World/Grass.js`: `subdivisions = 280`,
`surfaceIdeal = 2000`). Near the car — which is the only place density is
visible — this world is about two orders of magnitude behind.

The screenshots settle it. At the real gameplay camera:

- **GYM** renders as the car, one green ball, and a blank gradient.
- **FOCUS** renders as the car and one sign.
- **PROJECT ARCHIVE** renders as the car, a grey slab, and three small pines.

Those are not framing accidents; they are what the district respawns actually
put in front of the player.

### The same test, run on the reference

The brief's §39 asks for a complexity comparison rather than a pixel one. The
reference's **opening frame**, counted by hand off the capture, contains:

> a carpet of tall grass tufts over every unpaved surface · roughly ten trees in
> two or three species and sizes · massed bushes behind them · two lit lanterns ·
> two fences · a bench · a roofed signpost kiosk with a painted map on it ·
> three crates · the physical name in large 3D letters lying on tiled paving ·
> open water with a foam shoreline and a wooden pier · scattered rocks and
> debris · drifting leaf particles · an interactive marker

The equivalent frame here — the spawn — contains:

> a flat pale disc · eight cones · five white boxes · one billboard · two arrow
> signs · no grass · no water · no trees in frame

That is the gap, stated without a number attached. It is not a vegetation
deficit that a higher instance count fixes; it is an absence of **set
dressing, ground treatment and terrain incident** at the scale of the frame.

One further observation from driving the reference: its ground is *tiled and
edged*, with paving meeting planting at a visible border, and it undulates.
Here the ground is a single flat matte colour meeting another flat matte
colour at a painted circle.

## Macro composition

Read off the reference's own map UI:

- The island is **irregular and organic**, roughly square in footprint, with a
  ragged coastline and water pushing *into* the land as inlets and lagoons.
- A **serpentine race circuit fills nearly half the map** — one continuous
  ribbon with red/white curbing, not a small dedicated pad.
- The other half carries **every other activity, packed tightly**: winding
  sand-coloured paths, a dozen interactive markers, several small ponds, and
  vegetation in *masses* — thickets, treelines, shore planting — never an even
  scatter.
- One dark asphalt road with lane markings runs through the middle and ties
  the halves together.

Against that, this world is a **radial hub-and-spoke of flat circular plates**:
19 discs on a near-flat green plane, joined by straight roads, with large empty
lawns between them. The terrain is described in its own source as "a broad,
almost flat plain out to ~250 m" with "gentle rolling noise, ±1.4 m"
([`src/world/world/Terrain.ts:27-30`](./src/world/world/Terrain.ts#L27)).

That layout is the single thing most at odds with the brief's §33 ("avoid
hub-and-spoke roads where every activity sits at the end of an empty straight
road") and §6 ("do not preserve empty space without purpose").

### Where the discs come from

One field is doing three different jobs. `District.radius` is documented as
"discovery radius, and the footprint drawn on the map"
([`src/content/world.ts:88`](./src/content/world.ts#L88)) — but `Terrain` also
uses it to **flatten the ground**, out to `radius * 1.55`, tapering from
`radius * 0.72` ([`src/world/world/Terrain.ts:191`](./src/world/world/Terrain.ts#L191)).

The radii range from 28 m to 74 m. So:

- the `hub` flattens a **78 m-radius disc**,
- the `circuit` flattens a **111 m-radius disc**,
- and the nineteen plates together claim **123,176 m²** of flattened ground —
  a third of the entire island, and more than three times the total area of
  Bruno's world.

That is the mechanism behind both complaints at once: it is why the terrain is
flat, and why the map reads as circles on a lawn. Nothing in upstream flattens
anything remotely like this; areas there sit *on* shaped ground.

**The fix is to separate the three meanings**: a small (or absent) terrain
plate, a discovery radius that stays generous, and a map footprint drawn from
the real terrain. That one decoupling does more for the macro look than any
amount of added vegetation.

### What is genuinely better here, and should survive

The terrain is a **pure function**, not a texture — so `heightAt(x, z)` is
callable from landmark placement, prop scattering, the map and the minimap
without a raycast ([`src/world/world/Terrain.ts:14-21`](./src/world/world/Terrain.ts#L14)).
Upstream cannot do that; it has to sample a texture on the GPU and keep a
separate authored heightfield for physics, and the two disagree by design
(`Floor.js` sinks the visual floor by `-1.5·b` while `Snow.js` assumes `-2·b`).

Keep the pure function. Add upstream's R/G/B mask *channels* to it as further
pure functions, so paving, grass coverage and water depth become queryable the
same way.

The vehicle, suspension, camera and input model are also not in question and
are not touched by any action in this document.

## Migration plan

The brief's §40 asks for a plan rather than endless patching where the map
architecture is itself the blocker. It is the blocker. Here is the plan.

### Why a relayout, not a densification pass

Scattering more vegetation across 407,000 m² does not fix pacing, does not fix
the hub-and-spoke road topology, and costs performance everywhere to raise
density nowhere in particular. The footprint has to come down first, or every
density decision gets made twice.

### Why it is tractable

World positions are **centralised in two data files**:

| File | Role |
| --- | --- |
| [`src/content/world.ts`](./src/content/world.ts) | 19 districts, 83 landmarks, roads, ramps, 22 respawns — 137 coordinates |
| [`src/content/world-environment.ts`](./src/content/world-environment.ts) | circuit spline, lakes, river, waterfall, bridges, play spots, forest pockets — 23 coordinates |

Everything downstream is derived, not duplicated: `Terrain` builds height from
the road/district tables, `Ecology` scatters against `FOREST_POCKETS` and
`inlandWater()`, `Landmarks`/`Props` place from the landmark table, and the
mini-games build their arenas **relative to an anchor**, so their internal
geometry is scale-independent.

That means the relayout is an edit to two data files plus the terrain shaping
function — not an engine rewrite, and not a change to the vehicle.

### The one rule that makes this subtle

**Positions compress; objects do not.** Districts, roads, landmark footprints
and mini-game arenas are in real metres and must keep their size, or the car
stops feeling right. So this cannot be `x *= 0.42` — at that factor the 19
district plates would overlap. It is a genuine hand-authored relayout that
happens to reuse all existing content.

### Target

| | Now | Target | Reference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Extent | 720 m circle | irregular island inscribed in ~300 × 300 | 192 × 192 |
| Area | ~407,000 m² | ~90,000 m² | ~36,900 m² |
| Area per activity | ~13,100 m² | ~2,900 m² | ~2,800 m² |
| Terrain relief | ±1.4 m | ±8–12 m | authored heightfield |

A little larger than upstream, because this world carries more content: 19
districts, 12 play spots and 15 mini-games against upstream's 13 areas. The
*ratio* is what is being matched, and it lands within 5% of it.

### Shape

Adapting the reference's composition, with Alejandro's content:

- **One flank is the circuit.** Upstream gives roughly half the map to a
  serpentine ribbon with curbing. The existing spline becomes that ribbon
  instead of a pad tucked in a corner.
- **The rest is the content half**, with activities packed at reference
  spacing and joined by winding roads, loops and crossroads — not spokes.
- **Water is structural**: a ragged coastline with inlets, interior lakes, a
  river from a hill spring to the sea, bridges where roads cross it.
- **Relief is real**: a wooded ridge in the north feeding the waterfall, a
  valley, coastal dunes — so vegetation has somewhere to mass and jumps have
  somewhere to happen.
- **Districts stop being discs.** They become clearings and built-up areas
  bedded into terrain, with edges made of planting rather than a painted circle.

### Order of work

1. Relayout the two data files and reshape the terrain function.
2. Grass field, then composed vegetation. *(Highest visual return per line of
   code; see the density numbers above.)*
3. Near-field prop and set-dressing density, spawn area first.
4. Physical name, TNT, bowling, circuit replacement.
5. Remaining interactive areas, secrets, collectibles.
6. Map UI rasterised from the terrain function, so it cannot drift.
7. Full playtest, then re-audit this document.

## Parity tables

### Macro world, terrain and floor

The systems are ported honestly; the composition is not. Zones, Respawns and the Tracks ring buffer match or beat upstream, and the pure-function heightfield is a defensible improvement — heightAt is available to every placement system without raycasting. But this domain's actual subject, how the world reads and how full it feels, is far off. The playable area is 11x upstream's while carrying comparable content, so the emptiness is arithmetic. Terrain is a baked albedo canvas rather than a live shader data layer, so nothing samples the ground: no paving detail, no light bounce, no grass mask, no baked noises. Interior relief is about 3.4 m over a 720 m island — genuinely flat — and 19 circular plates, 11 of them at exactly y=0, sit on it. The floor is one static 204,800-triangle plane that is never culled, while upstream's 3,528-triangle camera-following trick is absent, and with no optimalArea five sizing numbers are hand-tuned independently. Vegetation covers 15% of the island at 8 m spacing; grass is 0.085 tufts per square metre. Fix the scale first; every other fix gets cheaper afterwards.

### Water

This domain is a genuine reimplementation, not a stub — analytic water regions, terrain-carved beds, per-body damping, wakes, splash audio, bridges and a waterfall all exist and several beat upstream. But it fails on the two things that actually define folio-2025's water. First, density: 1.97% of the island is inland water, in two rim lakes and one edge-hugging river, so the entire hub-and-spoke core is waterless. Second, surface fidelity: the shore band is one wind-independent sine at five times upstream's band spacing, the "noise" is a product of axis-aligned sines, rain does nothing but brighten a streak, there are no splash rings, no ice, and no ±0.013 waterline band on props or the car. Two outright defects compound it: the water shaders ignore scene fog while the 1600 m ocean plane extends 340 m past fogFar, and the river terminates 19 m outside WORLD_RADIUS as a ribbon floating ~4 m above dry ground, never reaching a sea whose own shoreline starts 3 m further out. Fix fog, the river mouth, the noise source and the ripple formula first — they are cheap and carry most of the look.

### Wind, weather and cycles

This domain has a good skeleton and almost no flesh. The day-cycle keyframes (Lighting.ts:51-61) are genuinely strong — nine stops, smoothstepped, colour-lerped — and the texel-snapped shadow target (Lighting.ts:250-255) is better engineered than upstream's. Everything downstream of the clock is thinner than it looks. Weather is five discrete states with binary rain/snow (Weather.ts:49-57), so sleet, drizzle and thaw cannot exist and there is no temperature, humidity, cloud or electric-field scalar for anything to read. Wind is a scalar plus an unnormalised, continuously rotating vector (Weather.ts:328) consumed by exactly two files; there is no shared gust field, so wind is invisible across precisely the empty green ground the layout already suffers from. Snow never settles, rain is silent, and lightning is a 0.55 exposure bump. Highest-value next pass, in order: continuous coupled Weather scalars; the shared wind-offset field plus four wind streaks; rain and wind on the audio bus; terrain whitening as snow accumulates; night fog pulled from 60 m to ~12 m. Those five recover most of the perceived gap.

### Rendering, instancing and quality tiers

The engine scaffolding is genuinely good — `Props.ts` beats upstream's `InstancedGroup` (scratch matrix, real dirty Set, interpolated sampling), the fixed-timestep `Ticker` is a defensible improvement, `Events` ordering is faithful, and the material registry keeps the scene at 7 textures and 138 draw calls. What is missing is the entire visual layer that makes folio-2025 look like folio-2025. There is no shared shading model: no light bounce, no core-shadow terminator, no albedo-tinted shadow, so every object reads as stock PBR sitting on the ground rather than painted into it. There is no bloom at all, so emissives are flat swatches and nothing glows at night. There is no camera-following grass field, and the canopy is solid icosahedra rather than alpha-cut quad shells. And the measured HIGH tier uses 5.7 ms of a 16.7 ms frame — the world is under-populated, not over-budget. Two outright defects: `sortObjects` is left at three.js' default, and a governor upgrade from LOW re-enables the shadow map while every mesh still carries `castShadow = false`.
